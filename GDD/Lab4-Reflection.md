**Lab 4 Reflection**

**1\. Does your high concept promise something your core loop actually delivers? Where is the gap, if any?**

Mostly yes. The high concept promises quick, easy-to-learn fights where each warrior still feels different, and the core loop delivers the quick and easy part, since there are only four moves and the feedback is instant. The gap is the "each warrior feels different" part. Right now every warrior shares the same punch, kick and block, so the only real difference is the special move. If the specials aren't different enough in the prototype, the warriors will feel the same, and that is the promise we need to work on next.

**2\. Which formal element was hardest to pin down for our game, and why?**

Rules and boundaries were the hardest. For rules, we had to decide how blocking and the special move actually work, because it's easy to say "block reduces damage" but harder to say by how much and what the special costs. For boundaries, we had to think about the camera and the edges of the stage, since the fighters need to stay on screen. It was easier for things like players and outcome, because a fighting game already has a clear way to win.

**3\. What did a peer's read-around make you see that you had missed?**

The read-around made us see that we wrote the document like we already knew the game, so some parts were clear to us but not to someone reading it fresh. Our high concept was one long sentence packed with the warriors, the duels and the god, and it was hard to picture the game from it. We also only explained the quick loop of pressing a button and seeing the result, and we forgot to say how that fits into the bigger loop of rounds, matches and the god boss. Reading it from another person's side helped us notice these gaps, and we fixed them in the revision.
